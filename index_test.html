A little about Ken Hollis and my career

This is not only for how my career went but hopefully some hints about a career in tech if you are considering going into this field. I have had several careers in my life. "If you stop learning, you start dying" is one of my mottos. Another is that if you are not advancing with the technology in 3 to 4 years (max) your skills will not be applicable to tech companies needs. Keep your skills relevant.

AI (Artificial Intelligence) was discussed as a theoretical. Networking, CyberSecurity, and Cloud Computing were not even imagined when I was getting my Math/CS degree. I had to learn those on the job. I managed to retire without being fired or laid off, I attribute this to luck and skill. Companies USED to have jobs for life, no longer. You are a commodity to be hired/fired/laid off.

Learn what you can and if you aren't advancing look for another job. The below shows how I advanced from one technology to another. I was willing to not settle into one job or a place and willing to move to another part of the country for a new job. You can do the same, and yes it is VERY scary every time.

I took jobs that I found interesting and kept learning from. Keeping my mind active and staying curious. Jobs where I was NOT "the smartest person in the room". I wanted to be challenged.

Eventually I was aiming for retirement, working with my investment advisor to have enough to retire on. In the end time is the one thing that no matter how rich you are you can't buy more of. I wanted experiences like travelling and riding my motorcycle. If you believe in anything after this life the only thing you will take with you are experiences and memories.

'I have never bought anything with money, everything I have was bought with pieces of time I sold from my life to a job that will never have paid enough when my time is up .' - Unknown

Before I graduated college I had the usual assortment of random jobs. Working in a restaurant cleaning dishes, as a veterinarian assistant, in a bank sorting credit card payments, operating the HP 3000 III mini-computers for the Richmond newspaper, programming for United States Geological Survey Hydrologic Survey , a lab assistant in the Virginia Commonwealth University (VCU) computer lab ( IBM 360/370 / Amdahl , HP 3000 III mostly).

My strongest impetus to get my degree was to get out of Richmond, Virginia. A city with low pay and the expectation that you would live with your parents. I knew with a Computer Science degree I would have the luxuries in life, like a roof over my head and food on the table :-)
