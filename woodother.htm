Free Woodworking Plans

Search Bing for free woodworking plans – always a reasonable starting point.

(A note on this page: the original version listed the free–plan directory sites of the early 2000s.  Every single one of them is now dead or worse – parked domains and ad farms.  Below is the current list of sites that are actually worth your time.  The lesson, as always: the Internet forgets, save local copies of any plan you care about.)

Ana White – A massive library of free furniture plans built around dimensional lumber and simple joinery.  Farmhouse tables, beds, bookshelves, storage.  This is where most people should start – complete cut lists, clear diagrams, beginner friendly.

Kreg Tool – free plans – Hundreds of free plans with step–by–step instructions and material lists.  Yes, Kreg wants to sell you their pocket hole jig, and yes the plans lean on pocket holes – but the plans are genuinely good, the jig is genuinely useful, and pocket holes are an honest entry point to joinery no matter what the hand–tool purists mutter.

Instructables – Workshop – Enormous user–contributed collection, beginner through advanced.  Quality varies because it is user–generated – read the comments on a plan before you cut wood, that is where the mistakes get documented.

Family Handyman – Tested plans with professional photography, plus general home improvement.

Wood Magazine and Popular Woodworking – The surviving magazines.  A mix of free and paid plans; the free ones are magazine–quality with proper measured drawings.

Woodgears.ca (Matthias Wandel) – In a category of his own.  An engineer who builds his own machines (bandsaws, box joint jigs, marble machines) out of wood and documents everything.  Some plans free, some paid, all of it worth reading just to watch how he thinks through a problem.

One caution that did not exist when I first wrote this page: a LOT of "free plans" links floating around Pinterest and image search are repins with the original source stripped off, or AI–generated filler sites wrapped in ads.  Trace any plan back to its original site before you trust the dimensions – read it with the same skeptical eye you should use on any other information (including mine).

Communities

The original version of this page pointed at the Usenet news group rec.woodworking – and if you know what Usenet is, we can be friends.  The conversation has since moved:

r/woodworking – The largest active community; the wiki and search are useful even if you never post.
Sawmill Creek – An old–school forum with deep expertise, closer in spirit to the Usenet days.  When you have a question about a specific technique or an old machine, this is where the people who actually know are.
