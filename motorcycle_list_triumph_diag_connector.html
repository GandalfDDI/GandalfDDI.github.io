Triumph Diagnostic Connector - 6-Pin (Red) vs 16-Pin (OBD-II) by Model

Compiled November 2026.

Triumph has never published a model-by-model list of which bikes have which diagnostic connector, and I could not find one anywhere else either. This table is assembled from tool vendors, adapter fitment lists and owner reports. Every row says where it came from and how much to trust it. If you have additions or corrections send me e-mail (see bottom of page).
The 30 second answer: pull the seat and look. A black, car-sized, 16-pin trapezoid is the old connector. A small, red, square 6-pin is the new one. Model year alone is not reliable - the change rolled through the range model by model between 2023 and 2026, and market differences are possible.

Here to turn off the service wrench? Skip to section 6.

1. The Story

  • Old connector - every fuel-injected Hinckley Triumph up to the changeover used the same 16-pin SAE J1962 socket found under the dash of a car. Unusual for a motorcycle maker, and convenient: any car OBD-II dongle plugged straight in.
  • New connector - the red 6-pin is the standardized motorcycle diagnostic connector (ISO 19689) that came in with Euro 5. Honda, Kawasaki, Suzuki and Yamaha use the same plug.
  • The change is not tied to "Euro 5" as such. Triumph sold Euro 5 bikes for years (2020-2023) with the old 16-pin still fitted. The red plug arrived with each model's next major update, starting with the Street Triple 765 for MY2023, and is close to universal on Euro 5+ bikes.
  • The electronics behind the plug did not change much. It is still CAN. A passive 6-pin to 16-pin adapter cable (about $10) puts you back where you were.
  • Both connectors are female on the bike side. The tool or adapter carries the male pins.
    • 16-pin: The harness end is a J1962 female socket, same as a car. HEX's Tiger 1200 guide describes the adaptor's male OBD-II plug mating with the bike's female OBD-II connector, and Cycle Terminal sells the J1962F female connector set as the one used on late model Triumphs.
    • 6-pin: Adapter cables for the red plug are built as 16-pin female to 6-pin ISO 19689 male, which makes the bike side the female half. The 16-pin female end of that cable then takes your existing dongle's male plug.

CAN pins on the 6-pin are from AiM's Triumph CAN documentation. I have not verified the power and ground pins, so they are left out on purpose.
  • Not every 6-to-16 adapter cable works. One owner's first Amazon cable would not talk to an OBDLink MX+ / TuneECU on a 2024 Tiger 900. A different cable did. Cables sold for Honda Euro 5 bikes are widely reported to work.
  • Two red-ish 6-pin plugs can live next to each other on some bikes. If the adapter will not mate, you have the wrong one.
  • The 16-pin has no latch. If you leave something plugged in (CAN accessory controller, etc.), tape the joint.
  • Connector does not equal tool support. Which plug the bike has tells you which cable to buy, not whether a given tool can reset the service indicator. See section 6.

6. Resetting the Service Indicator

This is why most people go looking for the diagnostic port in the first place. The reset is a software question, not a connector question - the 6-pin adapter cable only gets you connected.
Short version: TuneECU for Android with a Bluetooth OBDLink LX covers the widest range of bikes, including the ones DealerTool cannot reset. If the bike has the red plug, add a 6-to-16 adapter cable.

The pattern: DealerTool's no-reset list is very nearly the list of bikes with the red 6-pin plug, plus the newer-generation 660s, Speed Triple 1200 and Tiger 1200. Rule of thumb: red plug = DealerTool will not clear the wrench. Black 16-pin bikes other than those three families generally reset fine.
"Listed - reset expected" means the bike is in TuneECU's compatibility list and nothing says the reset is excluded, but I did not find an owner report for that exact model. "Yes" under DealerTool means the model is not on the no-reset list in DealerTool's FAQ (item 13, as read November 2026) - the FAQ only promises the reset on "most models with service indicators". That list has grown over time, so read it again before buying.
  • What to buy for TuneECU: an Android phone or tablet, an OBDLink LX (the one adapter that works on everything, and the only kind that works on the 2022+ Tiger 1200 besides the MX), a 6-pin to 16-pin adapter cable if the bike has the red plug, and the TuneECU subscription.
  • If the bike is not in TuneECU's list, it is not compatible. That is their rule, stated on the list itself. Check it before buying the adapter.
  • Cheap ELM327 clones are a gamble. The owner reports that work are nearly all OBDLink LX / MX / MX+.
  • Do not press "Reset Adaptions" when you mean "Reset Service". They are different functions. Resetting adaptions wipes learned values and on some models means going through a relearn procedure afterwards.
  • DealerTool reset ran but the wrench is still lit? On a supported bike, DealerTool says three things must match between the bike and the PC: the date (same format), the time (to the minute, same 12 / 24 hour format), and the distance units (miles vs km). Also use a current software version (6.57b or 6.60b at the time of writing).
  • The reset only clears the reminder. It does not do the service.
