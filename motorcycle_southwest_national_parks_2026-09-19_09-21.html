Trip Day Thirty-nine – Sunday, September 20, 2026: Tioga Pass and Tuolumne Meadows, Yosemite National Park (66 miles)

Woke up, made my coffee. I was sitting there drinking it and a young mule deer buck wandered by, he stood in the willows and aspens beside my camp at Lundy, his small rack and big mule-deer ears turned straight toward me. He didn't bolt or even flinch. He just looked over, decided I wasn't a threat, and went back to browsing a few yards from the tent:


Sun coming up and lighting up the canyon walls:


Rode over to Tioga Pass and explored Yosemite. This is Lembert Dome, at the east end of Tuolumne Meadows on the Tioga Road in Yosemite. It's a textbook roche moutonnée: granite bedrock shaped by the glacier that flowed west down the Tuolumne River canyon:


Looking southeast across the Dana Fork of the Tuolumne River, along the Tioga Road below Tioga Pass. The dark, rounded peak on the left is Mount Gibbs (12,779 ft), made of older metamorphic rock, and the pale granite on the right is Mammoth Peak at the north end of the Kuna Crest. The boulders in the foreground were dropped where they sit when the ice melted:


Panorama of Tioga Lake (9,638 ft). The rounded, rust-colored Tioga Peak (11,526 ft) on the left, and Mount Dana (13,061 ft), the second-highest peak in Yosemite, rising on the right:


Another picture from the road, amazing views around every corner:


Trip Day Forty – Monday, September 21, 2026: Lundy Lake over Tioga Pass, across the Golden Gate to Corte Madera, California (276 miles)

Morning at Lundy Lake Resort:


Tenaya Lake (about 8,150 ft) from the Tioga Road along the north shore, a basin scoured out by the same glacier that shaped Lembert Dome and the granite all around it. Named for Chief Tenaya of the Ahwahneechee, whose band was captured here by the Mariposa Battalion in 1851. Tenaya objected, he said that the lake already had a name, Pywiack, "lake of shining rocks":
