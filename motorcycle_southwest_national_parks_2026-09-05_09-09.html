Southwest National Parks Motorcycle Trip, Days 24 to 28. September 5 – 9, 2026.



Trip Day Twenty-four – Saturday, September 5, 2026: Santa Fe to Roswell, New Mexico (197 miles)

This is the ride from Santa Fe to Roswell, New Mexico – flat semi–arid desert going through towns that look half empty / abandoned. Saw lots of that in the SouthWest:
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6_qS84zuhmw&t=2953s

My wife had finished enjoying the portion of the vacation, so she jumped in her car, went to the airport and flew home (as planned). A garden to attend to and weekly Ping Pong to get back to :-). I was on my own for the rest of the trip. The ride to Roswell was to get closer to Carlsbad, the bonus is, of course, being in Roswell and see the 'UFO' tourist traps. Trip Day Twenty-six – Monday, September 7, 2026: Carlsbad Caverns and Guadalupe Mountains National Parks (126 miles)

Trip Day Twenty-seven – Tuesday, September 8, 2026: Carlsbad over Cloudcroft to Alamogordo, New Mexico (146 miles)

Riding from Carlsbad over Cloudcroft to Alamogordo is flat semi–arid desert for the first 1.5 hours but then you also get to go through mountains that are nice:
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AKWjU0CnkpE&t=18795s

You will be driving through the Lincoln National Forest:


I learned with the BMW K1600 that I needed to bring some kind of diagnostic tool and my PC on every long trip I made. When I bought the Triumph Tiger I also bought a Triumph Tiger diagnostic interface lead from dealertool.co.uk. I had my first scare with the Triumph Tiger on this trip. I went to start the bike at the Lincoln National Forest turnout, switched it off and back on again too quickly, check engine light came on. This was the first time I needed to use the diagnostic tool. Rode to the abode I was staying in, opened up the bike and connected the tool to my PC, saw that it was my user error, not the bikes. Cleared the error, the check engine light went away and it never came up again:


Trip Day Twenty-eight – Wednesday, September 9, 2026: White Sands National Park (42 miles)

This was a ride I was both looking forward to and had some concerns about. The Space Shuttle OV-102 (Columbia) mission STS-3 landed ONCE at White Sands. The technicians I worked with at KSC told stories of cleaning the quartz sand out of the aft for several flows after that, the sand got EVERYWHERE. So ... I decided to ride through the park hoping I wouldn't tump over and wouldn't get TOO much sand in the bike and just to get a feel for what all that white sand is like:
https://youtu.be/VADVBw-PTdM

It was, of course, beautiful. The sand was hard packed. While I was VERY careful about turns & riding but it all worked out fine. Be very careful about the route you take & how to get back out, once I was out in all that sand I realized how easily and quickly I could have gotten lost:


Brilliant white sand all around you. DON'T take any tho', not only is it illegal but there are people that believe it brings you a bad luck curse (as a side note if you REALLY want some quartz sand on Highway 70 one of the quartz dunes reaches out to the shoulder of the road from what I could see):
